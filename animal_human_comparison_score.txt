3. Cranio-Facial Angle
Some more advanced research and veterinary science uses the cranio-facial angle to classify dogs, especially in relation to brachycephaly. This measures the angle of the face from the forehead to the tip of the nose, which can differentiate between breeds with similar cephalic indexes but different facial profiles (e.g., a Pug vs. an "apple-headed" Chihuahua).

Cheeks (Dog)
In dogs, cheeks are a key part of the facial structure and are often defined by the tightness or looseness of the skin and the overall shape of the face. For breeds, the cheeks are an important breed standard.

Options:

Wrinkled/Loose: The cheeks are characterized by loose skin and prominent folds. This is a defining trait for certain breeds.

Well-Rounded/Full: The cheeks are broad and extend out, giving the face a more robust or "chubby" appearance. This is common in brachycephalic (flat-faced) breeds.

Tight/Flat: The skin on the cheeks is tight and close to the bone, with no wrinkles. This gives the face a more streamlined, sleek appearance.

Jowly: The skin on the cheeks is loose and hangs down around the jaw.

Gemini Context:
